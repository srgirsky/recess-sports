# Recess Sports — The Kids (World 1)

*Kids Roster · v0.7 · November 3, 2026*

**30 characters: 7 Tier 1 heroes and 23 Tier 2 neighborhood kids, 15 girls and 15 boys, covering all ten archetypes.** Companion to the Project Overview and the Archetypes.

Tier 1 kids carry custom rigs, screen time and the first toy line. Tier 2 kids share a modular rig and get one line each. Pick-rate data can promote any Tier 2 kid into Tier 1.

**Each character's identity is specific; each character's hook is about something else entirely** — a habit, a flaw, a stubbornness. Nobody here is a lesson.

## Tier 1 — The Seven

These carry custom rigs, screen time and the first toy line. **They are best guesses, not verdicts.** If pick-rate data says a neighborhood kid is outdrawing them, that kid gets promoted and one of these gets quietly benched.

### 1. Zaya Fontaine — the Speedster

**Age 8. Haitian-American.** The fastest thing in the neighborhood and she knows it.

- **Look:** Small, coiled, built like a spring. Ponytail streams straight back when she runs — the read at fifty feet. Perpetually in a half-crouch, like she's already leaving.
- **Hook:** Zaya does not speak during a game. Not one word. She's not shy — she's locked in, and she talks your ear off the second the last out is recorded.
- **Signature — Green Light:** She steals whenever she feels like it. Nobody calls it.
- **Story engine:** The prodigy problem. A team is not nine of her. Her arc is learning to slow down for people.
- **Why she's a toy:** The silhouette. Nothing else in the line reads like motion standing still.

### 2. Marisol "Mars" Delacruz — the Slugger

**Age 9. Dominican-American.** Tallest kid on the block. Swings so hard she falls over.

- **Look:** Long-limbed, broad-shouldered, with a bat visibly too big for anyone. Follow-through ends with her on the ground about a third of the time.
- **Hook:** Mars calls her shot every at-bat — the tree, the fence, the roof of the Nowak garage. She is wrong constantly and completely unembarrassed. Roughly once a game she is exactly right.
- **Signature — Moonshot:** Perfect timing and it's gone, no matter what the pitcher did.
- **Story engine:** All-or-nothing as a personality. She'd rather strike out swinging than take a walk.
- **Why she's a toy:** The called shot is a pose. That's the action figure.

### 3. Jae-Won "Jay" Park — the Fireballer

**Age 9. Korean-American.** Throws harder than anyone. Cannot admit when he's done.

- **Look:** Wiry, all elbows and knees, leg kick past his own shoulder. Pushes his glasses up between every pitch.
- **Hook:** Jay keeps a notebook on every batter in the neighborhood. He'll tell you he doesn't need it. He has never left the house without it.
- **Signature — Heater:** One maximum-velocity pitch per inning, at double the stamina cost.
- **Story engine:** His mechanical weakness is his character flaw. Stamina drains fast and Jay will not come out of the game.
- **Why he's a toy:** The leg kick. Best dynamic pose in the set.

### 4. Sione "Sonny" Fifita — the Wall

**Age 8. Tongan-American.** The biggest kid out there and the last one who'd ever use it.

- **Look:** Enormous. A wide, low, immovable block behind the plate in full catcher's gear — identifiable from across a store.
- **Hook:** Sonny apologizes to everyone — the runner he tagged, the pitcher, the ball. Softest voice from the largest kid, and he cannot be moved.
- **Signature — Brick House:** Anything within reach gets smothered.
- **Story engine:** Quietly stubborn. He doesn't move for anybody, at the plate or when something is unfair. The team's conscience, never raising his voice.
- **Why he's a toy:** Scale contrast. Sonny next to June on a shelf explains the whole line.

### 5. Dev Raghunathan — the Knuckler

**Age 9. Indian-American. Wheelchair racer.** Throws a pitch nobody can hit, himself included.

- **Look:** Racing chair — low, angled, built for speed. Powerful shoulders. Enormous grin roughly always.
- **Hook:** Dev has no idea where the ball is going, and thinks it's the funniest thing that has ever happened. Batters find this unbearable.
- **Signature — The Wobble:** Maximum randomness, maximum walk risk, maximum delight.
- **Story engine:** The chaos agent and comedy engine — and quietly the best pitcher in the neighborhood over a full season.
- **Why he's a toy:** The chair is a vehicle, and vehicles sell. But he's a hero because he's unhittable, not because he's brave.

### 6. June "Junebug" Halloran — the Table-Setter

**Age 6. Irish-American.** The youngest kid allowed to play, and she has never once struck out.

- **Look:** Tiny next to Sonny. Freckles, a helmet that slides, a bat choked up halfway.
- **Hook:** June cannot hit hard. She refuses to miss — nine fouls in a row with a straight face until the pitcher falls apart.
- **Signature — Never Miss:** Fouls them off instead of striking out. Indefinitely.
- **Story engine:** The youngest kid earning her spot — except she's already the most annoying out in the neighborhood.
- **Why she matters most:** **June is the four-year-old's character.** She always makes contact, so the youngest players have a hero who can't fail.

### 7. Ezra Bloom — the Junkballer

**Age 10. Jewish-American. Hattie's older brother.** Throws six pitches and not one of them is fast.

- **Look:** Tall and slouchy. Tzitzit below the jersey hem — visible, accurate, nobody's business. A windup so loose it looks like he forgot he was pitching.
- **Hook:** Ezra has never thrown a fastball and considers this a moral position. He talks like a retired pitching coach. He's right.
- **Signature — The Loop:** An extreme curve that starts outside the zone and lands in it. Batters always swing.
- **Story engine — the rivalry:** Velocity versus junk, Jay versus Ezra, two heroes on the same team who think the other is doing it wrong.
- **Story engine — Friday:** Ezra doesn't play Friday nights. Shabbat. No speech, no lesson — the best arm available is simply not there, and the team builds around it or doesn't.
- **Why he's a toy:** Sell him against Jay — the lazy windup next to the leg kick.

## How identity is handled

**Three honest channels, and no others.** Ethnicity lives in a name and a face. Religion shows up only as a name, something visible (Fatou's sport hijab, Guri's patka, Ezra's tzitzit) or a schedule constraint. Anything else is exposition, and that's the "lesson" failure mode.

**Every hook is about a habit or a flaw, never a heritage.** Yaz swings with her eyes closed. Musa learned his pitch off a video. Roya throws to the wrong base.

**The ethnicity-to-religion mapping is broken on purpose:**

- Four Muslims, four ethnicities: Fatou (Senegalese), Yaz (Bangladeshi), Musa (Somali), Nora (Bosnian). Muslim is not a skin tone.
- Amir Haddad is a Maronite Christian. Arab is not a religion.
- Roya Nazarian is a Persian Jew alongside the Ashkenazi Blooms. Jewish is not one culture.
- Guri Sandhu is Sikh; the patka under the helmet is the strongest silhouette in Tier 2.
- Two Native kids from two nations: Sam (Diné) and Josie (Ojibwe).

**No Native iconography, ever.** No team name, mascot, logo, chant or "spirit animal" in any world draws on Native imagery. The characters are in; the iconography stays out.

**The neighborhood clusters, deliberately.** Two Nigerian kids, two Mexican-American kids, two sets of siblings, three Jews, four Muslims. Real blocks repeat.

**Ezra doesn't play Friday nights** — a genuine draft constraint, same shape as the sibling cost. The Koufax echo is for parents.

**Ezra was promoted from Tier 2**, the system working as designed, at the cost of a seventh custom rig and toy mold. All four Muslim characters remain Tier 2 — a choice, not an accident.

## Siblings

Siblings get a bonus when played together, at the cost of two roster spots.

- **Roz & Rudy Achterberg** — Fireballer and Cannon. Arms run in the family.
- **Hattie & Ezra Bloom** — Table-Setter and Junkballer, now split across tiers: the famous brother and the underrated sister.

## What Tier 2 is not

No neighborhood kid gets an arc, a custom rig or screen time until the data says so. The moment one needs bespoke art, the roster math collapses.
